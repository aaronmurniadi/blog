Why I built OfxHost

I keep finding OpenFX plugins I want to try on a still photo, and then realizing the only way to run them is to open a full video host.

Resolve, Nuke, another app with a timeline I do not need. For one look on one RAW file, that felt backwards. So I built OfxHost: a small desktop app that loads a still, runs it through one or more OFX filter plugins, shows a preview, and exports the result. No timeline. No NLE. Just the image and the effects.

The itch

OpenFX is a real standard. Plugin authors ship film sims, analog dirt, grade tools, and more under that API. A lot of that work lives inside hosts aimed at motion.

If you are a photographer, or you just want to audition a look on one frame, you still pay the cost of a video app: project setup, clip import, render settings you do not care about.

I wanted the opposite shape. One loop, and nothing else:

  • Drop a photo in.
  • Pick a plugin, or a short chain of them.
  • Tweak the parameters.
  • Look at the result.
  • Export.

There was also a selfish reason. Effects like spektrafilm-ofx and ntsc-rs are interesting to me as still-image looks, not only as video treatments. Sitting in Resolve just to try grain and print simulation on a DNG was getting old.

What it does

  • Opens RAW camera files, plus TIFF, PNG, JPEG, and EXR. One photo per session.
  • Stacks OFX filter plugins in a node list you can reorder and bypass.
  • Previews at a few resolutions, so a slider move does not wait on the full file every time.
  • Exports to PNG, JPEG, or 16-bit TIFF.

Under the hood

The window is C++17 and Dear ImGui. LibRaw reads the camera files, libtiff, libwebp, and JPEG XL handle the rest, and Little CMS 2 does colour. GLFW is fetched by CMake, so ./build.sh is the whole build on macOS and Linux. Windows links the same libraries statically through vcpkg and ships as one file.

Colour management is practical, not a full ACES suite. Plugin support means “filters that behave like still processors,” not every OFX context under the sun. If something needs a proper timeline or a multi-clip graph, use a real NLE. Those limits are the point: they keep the app small enough to open for one frame.

The self-test is its own binary, so a shipped build can check itself without a window, and --selftest forwards to it: 

build/OfxHost.app/Contents/MacOS/OfxHost --selftest

Install

Releases carry a package for each platform:

  • macOS: OfxHost-macOS-arm64.dmg or OfxHost-macOS-x86_64.dmg. The app is ad-hoc signed, so right-click it and choose Open on the first launch.
  • Windows: one self-contained .exe. No installer, no console window.
  • Linux: OfxHost-Linux-x86_64.AppImage. Install zenity or kdialog if the Open and Export dialogs do not appear.

Plugins load from the platform OFX directory, and from any path in OFX_PLUGIN_PATH. On macOS, that is /Library/OFX/Plugins: 

sudo mkdir -p /Library/OFX/Plugins
sudo cp -R path/to/*.ofx.bundle /Library/OFX/Plugins/

Restart the app once the plugins are in place. If none are found, the status bar says so. Tested on stills so far: spektrafilm-ofx, ntsc-rs, and purzOS. Other OFX filter plugins should work too.

Who I hope it helps

Photographers and stills people who want OFX looks without learning a video editor first. Plugin authors who want a quick host for testing a filter on real photos. Anyone curious about film sims, VHS-style tools, or other OFX toys, and who would rather spend five minutes in a small app than twenty setting up a project they will delete later.

If that is you: grab a release from GitHub, drop an image in, and see what your installed OFX plugins do. The project is BSD 3-Clause, and issues and PRs are welcome. If a plugin works well on stills and is not in the tested table yet, that entry is a one-line change.

I built this because I needed it. I am putting it out because I suspect a few other people needed the same shortcut.
